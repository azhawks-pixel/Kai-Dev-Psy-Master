Kai’s Story · Developmental Psychology

Final assignment & assessment guide

Complete ten simulation decisions with brief notes: five as Kai’s parent and five as adult Kai. The simulation automatically records ages, options, decisions, results, and developmental observations. Two or three sentences across the note fields per decision is sufficient.

Part 1 · Parenting reflection

Write approximately 500–650 words, excluding references. Analyze at least three parenting decisions using at least three distinct concepts from the course textbook. Explain the concepts and apply them to specific story evidence. Address physical, cognitive, and social-emotional development across the section. Identify a choice to reconsider and a choice to retain. Evaluate context, uncertainty, continuity or change across childhood, and what you learned.

Part 2 · Adult-life reflection

Write approximately 500–650 words, excluding references. Analyze at least three adult decisions with at least three distinct textbook concepts and specific story evidence. Address all three developmental domains across the section. Identify a choice to reconsider and one to retain. Discuss context, uncertainty, and connections across ages. Explain what changed when you made decisions as Kai rather than as the parent.

Sources and submission

Use the course textbook plus at least two peer-reviewed journal articles across the assignment. Cite them in the reflections and provide a shared APA reference list. Articles may support either or both sections, but both articles must contribute meaningfully. Submit the downloaded decision record and both reflections with references to Canvas. If writing everything in the simulator, one downloaded text file includes all parts. If writing separately, submit the reflection document alongside the record. The app does not submit work.

Word counts are guidance for focused analysis. No additional theory paragraph is required for each decision note. You may reuse a concept in the adult section when it is accurately applied to new evidence. The simulation’s topic suggestions are not substitutes for reading and citing sources.

Assessment guide · 100 points

Use these criteria to check the decision record and both reflections. Points reward demonstrated analysis, not a particular life choice or a favorable story result.

Performance levels

Exceeds expectations (90–100% of each criterion’s points): Accurate, specific, and complete evidence of the criterion; connections are explained with appropriate limits and detail.

Meets expectations (80% to below 90% of each criterion’s points): Substantially fulfills the criterion; minor omissions or limited explanation do not undermine the main analysis.

Partially meets expectations (60% to below 80% of each criterion’s points): Some relevant evidence, but important omissions, generic claims, or weak connections limit fulfillment.

Does not meet expectations (0% to below 60% of each criterion’s points): Missing, substantially incomplete, or inaccurate evidence; the criterion is not adequately demonstrated.

Use the lower bound inclusively and the upper bound exclusively, except 100% is included. These ranges cover every score. For a 20-point criterion, the levels are 18–20, 16 to below 18, 12 to below 16, and 0 to below 12. Score each criterion on the quality of its evidence.

Apply, do not just name.

“This is self-efficacy” names a concept. A stronger analysis explains what self-efficacy means, identifies a particular change in Kai’s task-specific confidence or behavior, and explains how that evidence fits the concept. Acknowledging another possible explanation improves the analysis.

Source requirement: your textbook plus two peer-reviewed journal articles across the two reflections. At least three concepts are required in each reflection; the same concept may be reused in the other reflection when accurately applied to different evidence. No new paid resource is required; use available library access or open articles.

Final submission: decision-record text file plus both 500–650-word reflections and an APA reference list. If all writing and the reference list are entered here, one download contains them. Otherwise, submit your separate reflection document alongside the record.

Instructor scoring guidance

Use the stated criteria and performance levels, rather than previous class grades, as the standard. The record categories assess decisions and brief notes; do not require ten theory essays. The two theory categories assess conceptual accuracy and evidence-based application. The synthesis category assesses reconsideration, context, change over time, and learning; do not deduct twice for the same error.

  • Descriptive: “I chose school support because it would help.” This records a reason but does not yet demonstrate theory application.
  • Applied: “Kai requests directions before rehearsal after reading support. This behavior is consistent with greater task-specific self-efficacy because Kai anticipates being able to participate with preparation.” Evaluate the student’s definition and source support as well as the connection.
  • Critical: “The request is consistent with self-efficacy, but access to scripts, repeated practice, and a supportive teacher could also explain it. The story does not establish that one parenting choice caused later confidence.” This adds an alternative explanation and a limit to inference.

These are scoring illustrations, not complete model answers. Look for comparable reasoning across different defensible choices. Do not reward favorable fictional outcomes, a particular education or family path, length alone, or the number of terms named.

For calibration, instructors should independently score a small set of de-identified responses by criterion, compare differences, and document the evidence that distinguishes levels before grading a full section. A criterion-level score must be justified by demonstrated work. The app checks for entered text, not academic sufficiency; separate-document selections are intentions, not completed assignments.

Source changes from the previous rubric are explicit: this revision requires three sources total (textbook plus two peer-reviewed articles), replacing the previous requirement for three peer-reviewed references. Confirm this revised requirement in the Canvas assignment and rubric before use. The point weights remain 15 + 20 + 15 + 20 + 20 + 10 = 100.
